Aerospace Battery Testing

A practical guide to battery testing for aerospace programs — covering qualification standards, environmental simulation, abuse testing, long-duration cycling, and the data traceability requirements that accompany flight-critical certification.

APPLICATIONS

Understanding Aerospace Battery Testing

Flight-critical power demands the most rigorous test programs in the industry — and the data to prove it.

OVERVIEW

What Makes Aerospace Battery Testing Different

Aerospace battery testing operates under constraints that do not exist in most commercial programs: mandatory qualification standards, stringent traceability requirements, extreme environmental envelopes, and failure consequences that justify unusually long and expensive test campaigns. A battery that powers an aircraft emergency system, a satellite bus, or an unmanned aerial vehicle must be proven — not assumed — capable across every condition it will encounter in service.

The test program must produce data that satisfies both the engineering team and the certifying authority. That means procedures, limits, and results must be documented with the same rigor applied to the hardware itself.

PLANNING

Defining the Aerospace Test Objective

Aerospace programs typically layer multiple test objectives across a single campaign. Defining each clearly before starting prevents the common problem of discovering mid-program that the data collected does not meet the standard being submitted against.

  • Test objective: Qualification, acceptance, life demonstration, or failure mode characterization?
  • Control variables: Which parameters are fixed — current, voltage, temperature, pressure, cycle count?
  • Measured outputs: Capacity, energy, impedance, temperature rise, and any auxiliary inputs required by the standard?
  • Data resolution: What logging frequency and triggered record conditions satisfy the certifying authority's data requirements?
  • Operational risk: Which safeties, interlocks, and abort criteria protect the DUT, the equipment, and the test facility?
WORKFLOW

How Aerospace Battery Testing Works in Practice

Aerospace programs move through a structured sequence that emphasizes traceability at every step:

  1. Define the DUT, operating envelope, and acceptance criteria aligned to the applicable standard.
  2. Build the procedure to enforce the required charge and discharge profiles, rest periods, temperature conditions, and end conditions.
  3. Assign channels, configure auxiliary temperature inputs, and associate environmental chambers where required.
  4. Enable digital I/O interlocks and any abuse test triggers needed by the test plan.
  5. Execute and monitor in real time; confirm each step transitions correctly before leaving the lab.
  6. Export and archive data in the format required for the certification package.
Traceability note: Aerospace programs typically require that procedure files, calibration records, and data exports be version-controlled and archived together. The test system's data file links directly to the procedure name and start timestamp, supporting this chain of evidence.
APPLICATIONS

Where Aerospace Battery Testing Adds Value

The aerospace market spans a wide range of platforms and power requirements, but the test infrastructure challenge is consistent: the same cell chemistry may need to be proven across radically different mission profiles.

Platform Primary Test Focus
Commercial aviation (DO-311A) Qualification cycling, overcharge, short-circuit, and venting characterization
Low Earth orbit satellites LEO cycle simulation (90-min period), depth-of-discharge optimization, multi-year life demonstration
Defense / UAV MIL-spec acceptance testing, wide-temperature performance, pulse discharge capability
Launch vehicles High-rate discharge validation, vibration-concurrent electrical monitoring, post-shock capacity verification
KEY REQUIREMENTS

What Aerospace Programs Demand from Test Systems

  • Environmental integration: Chamber association ties temperature conditions directly to the test procedure, allowing the software to sequence electrical and thermal steps together and log chamber temperature alongside cell measurements.
  • Thermocouple and thermistor monitoring: Up to 48 auxiliary temperature inputs per channel capture surface temperature at multiple locations simultaneously, with thermocouple end conditions capable of triggering test abort if any point exceeds the configured safety limit.
  • High-resolution data logging: Time-based, event-based, and derivative-triggered record conditions ensure data density is concentrated at transitions — charge termination, rest recovery, temperature events — without generating unmanageable file sizes during stable plateaus.
  • Digital I/O interlocks: External safety signals — pressure relief indicators, facility fire suppression triggers, test stand abort lines — can be wired as digital inputs that immediately halt the test when asserted.
  • Long-duration unattended operation: Procedures with nested loops, global channel safeties, and automatic problem-state handling allow multi-week or multi-month programs to run with minimal operator intervention.
BENEFITS

Benefits for Aerospace Engineers and Program Managers

  • Certification-ready data: Timestamped, procedure-linked data files provide the chain of evidence needed for FAA, ESA, or DoD submissions.
  • Repeatable test execution: Automated procedures eliminate operator-introduced variation across shifts, sites, and multiple test articles.
  • Early anomaly detection: Real-time monitoring and derivative end conditions catch unexpected temperature or voltage behavior before it becomes a safety event or a data loss.
  • Scalable infrastructure: Multi-channel systems support concurrent testing of qualification units, flight units, and life-test articles on the same platform.
  • Long-horizon program support: Procedures, calibration records, and archived data remain accessible and re-runnable across the full life of a certification program.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the test system handle a fault during an unattended overnight test?

Global channel safeties and procedure-defined end conditions move the channel to a rest or open-circuit state and log a Problem status when a limit is exceeded. The test pauses safely, preserves the data collected to that point, and waits for operator review before resuming — without requiring a manual watchperson.

Can a single procedure cover the full qualification campaign?

Yes, within procedure length limits. Nested loops, subroutine calls, and Advanced Cycle steps allow complex multi-phase programs — formation, capacity characterization, life cycling, and end-of-life capacity check — to be encoded in a single procedure with automatic transitions.

How is temperature monitoring integrated into the electrical test record?

Thermocouple and thermistor auxiliary inputs assigned to the test channel are logged to the same data file as voltage, current, and capacity. No manual correlation between separate instruments is needed — the complete thermal and electrical history of each cycle is in a single exportable record.
